What actually happens at a family session

By Deep Arora · Still & Golden Photography · Cranbourne East & South-East Melbourne

The worry I hear most before a family session isn't about price or timing, it's "my kids will not sit still for this." Good. Sitting still is not the job. Here is how the hour actually runs, and why the kids who won't sit still usually produce the best photos of the lot.

At your place, or outdoors

A Golden family session works in two settings, and both are about keeping everyone comfortable. At home, we use the rooms with the best light, the couch everyone piles onto, the backyard, the front step. Outdoors, it's a park or beach you already know, somewhere the kids have room to move. If you're not sure which suits your family, we work it out together when you book, there's no wrong answer, just the one that feels more like you.

The first few minutes are deliberately uneventful. I get the light sorted in my head, the kids suss me out, and nobody is asked to smile at anything yet.

How I direct (gently)

I don't line families up and ask for cheese. Instead I give you small, easy things to do, walk towards me holding hands, everyone onto the rug, whisper something ridiculous in your partner's ear, see who can make dad laugh first. The prompts are scaffolding; the photos happen in the reactions.

The prompt is never the photo. The photo is the half-second after the prompt, when everyone forgets there's a camera.

If something isn't working, we drop it and move on within a minute. There's no shot so important that it's worth a standoff with a four-year-old.

What the kids typically do

Every age has its pattern, and after enough sessions none of them surprise me:

  • Toddlers treat the session as a sport. They warm up slowly, give us a brilliant ten-minute window in the middle, and are done before the hour is. We plan around that window, not against it.
  • Primary-schoolers start shy and end as creative directors, suggesting poses. Some of their ideas are genuinely excellent and make the gallery.
  • Babies come along for the ride, held, fed when needed, photographed in the arms of whoever's most popular that day.

Someone will have a grizzle at some point. That's a normal Tuesday with kids, not a failed session, we pause, they reset, and we carry on. As a dad of two I'd be suspicious of any family that made it through an hour without one.

What gets photographed

The hour produces a mix, and it's the mix that makes the gallery feel like your family rather than a photo shoot. The whole crew together, obviously, but also the kids on their own, each parent with each kid, the two of you while the kids orbit around your legs. Nobody has to decide these combinations in advance; they emerge as the hour moves, and we fit more in than you'd think because nothing is set up from scratch.

How the hour feels

Shorter than you expect, and easier. Most families settle in within the first fifteen minutes, the middle stretch is where the relaxed, unguarded photos come from, and we finish before anyone's patience does. You'll spend most of it interacting with each other, not with me.

One practical note: what you wear matters more to the final photos than most people expect, and it doesn't mean matching outfits. Soft neutrals and texture beat logos and loud prints every time. There's a full walkthrough in what to wear for your family photo session.
