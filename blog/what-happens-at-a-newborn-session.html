Newborn Photography

What actually happens at a newborn session

By Deep Arora · Still & Golden Photography · Frankston & South-East Melbourne

If you've never had a photographer in your home — especially in those first hazy weeks with a newborn — the whole idea can feel like one more thing to be nervous about. So here is the entire ninety minutes, exactly as it tends to unfold, including the feeds, the settling breaks and the bit where nobody performs at all.

When I arrive

I turn up light: a camera, a bean bag, and a small flash with an umbrella — that's the whole kit. The bean bag gives your baby a soft, safe spot for the detailed close-ups (the flaky skin, the tiny fingers, the little lips), and the flash only comes out for those detail shots when the window light needs a hand. There's no backdrop stand, no elaborate themed props or staged set-ups — newborn sessions with me are documentary, shot mostly with the natural light your home already has. The first ten minutes aren't photography at all: I say hello, have a look at the light in your bedroom and living room, and we chat while your baby does whatever your baby is doing.

You don't need to have the house ready for me. I only ever use two or three corners of a home, and the rest can look exactly like a house with a newborn in it — because it is one. If you're wondering how little preparation is genuinely required, I've written it up in preparing your home for a newborn session. The short version: please don't tidy.

The session follows the baby, not a shot list

Ninety minutes sounds long until you meet a newborn's schedule. Feeds, nappy changes, settling — all of it is built into the session rather than squeezed around it. A days-old baby keeps their own time, and the length of a Gathered session exists precisely so nobody has to hurry any of it.

In practice that means we work in short bursts. When your baby is settled and content, I'm photographing. When they need a feed, we stop — and I mean properly stop, camera down, while you do what you need to do. Some of the best frames of the whole session happen straight after those pauses, when everyone's shoulders have dropped again.

There is no such thing as a newborn session where the baby cooperates for ninety minutes straight. The session is designed around that fact, not in spite of it.

What you (and any siblings) actually do

Mostly, you just be parents. I might ask you to sit on the bed with the baby on your chest, or stand by the window together, or simply keep doing whatever you were doing — a feed on the couch, a slow lap of the hallway. I'll guide you into good light and then let the real stuff happen inside it.

There's no stiff posing and nothing to get right. If your toddler wants to be involved, they can be — a hand on the baby's head, a careful lie-down next to them, thirty seconds of fascination before they wander off to their toys. That's all completely normal, and honestly the wandering-off photos are usually the honest ones. If a sibling needs to take a break and watch something in the next room, the session rolls on without a flicker.

And you don't need to be camera-ready. You're in your own home, in comfortable clothes, and the photos are about the four of you, not about anyone's hair.

What gets photographed

The wide story and the small one. The wide story is your family as it is right now: the three or four of you on the bed, the nursery you spent months putting together, the couch where the whole pile lands at 6am. The small one is the details that disappear within weeks — the curl of fingers around yours, the flaky newborn skin, the way they fit along one forearm.

  • The baby on their own — settled on the bed or in the cot, in window light.
  • The baby with each parent — held, rocked, dozing against a shoulder.
  • Everyone together — the whole family, siblings and pets included if they're willing.
  • The details — hands, feet, ears, the grip that startles you with its strength.

Afterwards

I pack up in minutes and leave your house exactly as I found it. Within two weeks your edited gallery arrives — a private online gallery of 30 or more images, ready to download and share. Most families tell me they look through it once quickly, then again properly that night when the baby's finally asleep.

If you're still pregnant and reading ahead: the timing question is its own topic, and it's covered in when to book newborn photos. The essence is that newborn sessions happen any time in the first four weeks after birth, we pencil in a date around your due date, and it simply moves with your baby if they arrive early or late.
