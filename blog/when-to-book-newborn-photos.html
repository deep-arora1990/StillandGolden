When is the best time to book newborn photos?

By Deep Arora · Still & Golden Photography · Cranbourne East & South-East Melbourne

It's one of the first questions expecting parents ask, and the answer surprises most of them: there's no set time. Whenever you're ready is the right time. At two weeks or twelve, it's just a different phase, and they're still your little newborn. Here's how booking works, why you can ignore the "two week rule", and what to do if your baby is already here.

Book while you're pregnant, if you like

Plenty of families book before the birth. Not because the session has to happen at a certain age, but because it takes the job off the post-birth to-do list. Once your baby arrives, you'll have roughly nine hundred other things competing for your attention, and the last thing you'll feel like doing at 3am is researching photographers.

Booking ahead also holds a spot for you. I only take on a few newborn sessions each month, because every one has to stay flexible around an unpredictable due date.

A newborn booking is really a pencilled-in promise: "whenever you're ready after your baby arrives, we'll make this happen."

Here's how it works with me: you book during pregnancy, I note your due date, and we pick a tentative week. When your baby arrives, you send a message when you're ready (no pressure, no countdown) and we confirm the actual date. Early arrival, late arrival, unexpected caesarean recovery: the date simply moves with your family.

No set time, just different phases

You may have read online that newborn photos "must" happen in the first two weeks. That rule comes from posed studio photography, where photographers rely on very sleepy, curly babies for moulded poses.

In-home, documentary-style sessions don't need any of that. I'm photographing your baby being your baby: feeds, cuddles, naps, tiny details, and the way you hold them. That works beautifully at day 5, week 6 or week 12. No set time means:

  • Mum gets time to recover. Birth is enormous. If week one feels too raw, we wait.
  • Feeding has time to settle. Many families find things noticeably calmer once feeding finds a rhythm.
  • There's room for real life. Jaundice checks, sleepless stretches, visiting grandparents: there's room for all of it.

Early on you get the smallness, the newborn skin, the curled-up sleeping positions, the way they disappear into a parent's arms. A little later you get more alert eyes, more expressions, and maybe the first smiles. Different phases, both worth keeping.

"Our baby is already here. Is it too late?"

No. Get in touch whenever you're ready and I'll do everything I can to fit you in. Last-minute newborn sessions happen more often than you'd think.

Two weeks or twelve, it's still a newborn session, just a different phase. Babies who have found their smile, their chub and their personality photograph wonderfully. The best time for photos is always the season you're actually in.

A quick timing checklist

  • Still pregnant: book whenever you like, and I'll pencil in a date around your due date.
  • Baby is already here: get in touch when you're ready; we'll likely make it work, whatever their age.
  • Two weeks or twelve: still a newborn session, just a different phase.
