First birthdays, gloriously messy

One year of your little person, celebrated with both hands in the icing.

The first birthday is a big one — for your baby, and honestly, for you. You made it through the newborn fog, the sleep regressions and a year of firsts. A cake smash session marks the milestone the way a one-year-old would want to: with cake, chaos and absolutely no table manners.

I'm Deep, a dad of two based in South-East Melbourne. These sessions happen in your own home, so your little one is on familiar turf — no strange studio, no pressure to perform. Just a simple, beautiful setup, their favourite people close by, and a cake with their name on it.

How the hour runs

  • The clean portraits first — a few frames of your one-year-old looking their birthday best, plus family cuddles while everyone's still icing-free.
  • Then, the cake. We put it down, step back and let curiosity take over. The first cautious poke, the first fistful, the full two-handed demolition — that arc is the session.
  • The gloriously messy finale — icing-covered grins, cake on the floor and, if you like, a splash-bath wind-down to finish.

What's included

  • 1-hour session in your own home — relaxed pace, with plenty of room for snack breaks and warm-up time.
  • 30+ edited images, delivered within 2 weeks through a private online gallery.
  • Setup and clean-up help. I bring the patience and a hand with the mess — you won't be scraping icing off the floor alone.
  • Family in the frame. Parents, siblings, grandparents — anyone who wants to celebrate (or be lovingly smeared with cake) is welcome.

About the cake

For dietary and allergen reasons, parents provide their own cake — that way you know exactly what your little one is eating, whether that's a classic sponge, a refined-sugar-free bake or an allergy-friendly recipe.

Any cake works, but a simple single-tier cake with soft icing smashes best — buttercream is easier on little hands than fondant. Light-coloured icing photographs beautifully, and a smash cake doesn't need to be big or expensive. Once you book, I'll send through a short guide with easy cake and outfit tips.

What to expect with a one-year-old

Some babies dive straight in. Some burst into tears the moment the cake lands in front of them. Both are completely normal — and as a dad of two, I've seen every version. We go at your child's pace, pause whenever they need a cuddle or a snack that isn't cake, and there's no time pressure. The hesitant ones usually come around; the criers usually end up with the funniest gallery.
