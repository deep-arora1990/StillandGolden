Why in-home?

Because packing up a days-old baby, a nappy bag and two exhausted parents to drive to a studio is nobody's idea of a good morning — and because extended family photos shouldn't require herding grandparents into an unfamiliar room. At home, everything you need is within reach — the change table, the feeding chair, the kettle. Everyone stays settled in a familiar space, and you get to be comfortable in your own environment.

It also makes the photos yours. The nursery you spent months putting together. Your bed, where the whole family piles in at 6am. These backdrops mean something now, and they'll mean even more in twenty years.

When should you book?

For newborn sessions: whenever you're ready. There's no set time for newborn photos. At two weeks or twelve, it's just a different phase, and they're still your little newborn. That lets us work around your recovery, feeding routines and how you're all travelling.

If you're still pregnant, you can book now. I pencil in a tentative date around your due date, then we confirm once your baby arrives. If bub comes early or fashionably late, the date simply moves with them.

For everything else — extended family visits, milestones, a houseful of cousins — any date that suits you works. A couple of weeks' notice is usually plenty, and weekends go first.

What to expect on the day

  • 90 relaxed minutes at your place — no rushing, no rigid shot list.
  • The full gallery, usually 60+ edited images, delivered through a private online gallery within 2 weeks.
  • Everything at your pace. For newborns, feeds, cuddles and settling breaks are built in, not squeezed around. Bigger groups get the same unhurried treatment.
  • Mostly natural light. I work with the light from your windows — usually a bedroom, the couch and the nursery. For newborn close-ups and detail shots I bring a small flash with an umbrella and a bean bag, so the tiny fingers-and-toes frames come out soft and clean. No themed props or backdrops beyond that.
  • The whole family welcome. Toddlers, pets, grandparents — the more of your real life in the frame, the better.
  • No need to tidy up. I only need two or three corners of your home. The rest can look lived in, because it is.
