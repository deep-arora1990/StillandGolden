The photographer

A fellow parent, behind the lens

Still & Golden is one person with a camera, a lot of patience, and two small kids at home.

Hi, I'm Deep — a dad of two based in Cranbourne East, and the person behind Still & Golden. I have a three-year-old who keeps me on my toes and a brand new baby who has completely stolen my heart (and my sleep).

I've had a camera in my hands since I was five or six, when my dad first let me use his Yashica MF2. And I still love going through the photo album in the cupboard at my parents' place. The photos in it aren't perfect, but the feeling is. There's love and joy, smiles and frowns, everyday moments just as they happened. That's what I want to make for you and your family.

Becoming a dad made it personal. My first daughter's newborn photos were carefully posed. At my second daughter's session, the photographer caught moments we didn't even know she'd taken, and those are the ones we keep going back to. I started Still & Golden on parental leave this year, after photographing her birth announcement myself.

I know firsthand how fast these early days go. My approach is gentle and unhurried — I work at your baby's pace, in a space that feels comfortable for your whole family. What you'll take home are warm, honest images that feel like you.

Why I shoot the way I do

My style is natural and candid: real moments, photographed as they happen in your family.

For my main sessions there's no studio. I come to your home, or to a place that means something to you and your family, because a studio can't give you the kitchen floor on a Saturday morning, the nursery you spent months putting together, or your bed where the whole family piles in at 6am. The backdrop of your photos should mean something. In twenty years it will mean even more.

The one exception is the occasional seasonal mini session day, when I hire a studio to escape the unpredictability of Melbourne weather.

I don't spend the session arranging people into poses, because posed isn't what you'll miss. What you'll miss is the scrunched-up laugh, the way your toddler runs — never walks — down the hallway, how impossibly small your newborn looks in their dad's arms. Those things don't happen on command. They happen when everyone forgets I'm there, which is more or less the entire method.

The other reason is more personal. In most families there's one person who takes every photo and appears in almost none — and I know how that story ends, because one day your kids will go looking for a photo of you. My job, more than anything else, is to make sure it exists.

What a session with me is like

The unspoken question before any family session isn't "will the photos be good?" — it's "will this be awkward?" So here it is plainly:

  • Nothing is required of you. No tidying, no outfit rules, no rehearsing the kids.
  • The first ten minutes are meant to be nothing. The kids case the place, you hover, I stay out of the way.
  • You'll be asked to do something ordinary — make toast, read the usual book, shoot hoops out the back.
  • Around minute twenty, everyone forgets I'm there. That's the whole method.
  • Someone will wander off, and nobody will chase them.
  • Nobody says "everyone look here." If it feels staged, I've done it wrong.
  • Two weeks later it's done — and you didn't have to manage any of it.
